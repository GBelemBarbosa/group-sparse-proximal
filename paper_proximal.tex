\begin{abstract}
We study composite minimization of a smooth objective plus a group-\(\ell_0\)
regularizer, subject to both an upper and a \emph{lower} bound on the number of
active groups. The lower bound models diversification and coverage constraints
that the classical cardinality-constrained formulation cannot express. The main
result is a complete characterization of the proximal mapping of the resulting
discontinuous regularizer, extending Theorem~3.2 of
\citeauthor{beck2018} (\citeyear{beck2018}). Each group constraint is required
to exclude the origin, otherwise the proximal mapping may be empty even when
every group set is closed. Under that restriction the mapping is
nonempty, and we give an explicit description of every proximal point together
with an algorithm that computes one. The proof extends their comparison of
groupwise gains to the case in which the lower bound is active. A different
penalty may be attached to each group. The mapping is then obtained by
subtracting twice that penalty from the corresponding gain and comparing the
resulting vector with zero, with the proof naturally following from the single 
penalty case.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
group sparsity; \(\ell_0\) regularization; proximal mapping; cardinality
constraints; composite optimization.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Group sparsity comprises optimization problems whose decision variable is
partitioned into disjoint blocks (groups), and in which one wishes to limit or
penalize the number of nonzero groups. The structure arises whenever
explanatory variables are highly correlated and must be treated jointly, or
when the model itself imposes a predetermined grouping
\cite{yuan2006,simon2013,beck2018}. Standard applications include
portfolio construction,
imaging and signal processing, total-variation denoising, compressed sensing
\cite{donoho2006}, and low-rank approximation; see \cite{beck2018} and the
references therein.

The generic proximal mapping of \cite{beck2018} covers a penalty on the number
of active groups together with an \emph{upper} bound \(s\) on that number. We enlarge the class slightly by introducing a \emph{lower}
bound \(l\) as well. The extra constraint controls the maximal sparsity of a
solution and is useful whenever a minimum level of diversification is
required: one should not concentrate a portfolio on a handful of sectors
\cite{brodie2009,beck2018}. Even when the floor is only a target rather
than a hard modelling constraint, specifying \(l\) is more convenient than
tuning \(\lambda\) until the recovered support meets a desired minimum size.
The constraint enforces the cardinality directly, and the grid of candidate
penalties can be cut accordingly. 

The penalty itself need not be the same for
every group. A vector \((\lambda_i)_{i=1}^m\) charges group \(i\) its own
price, which a single \(\lambda\) cannot express, as one group may be more costly
to activate than another. \Cref{sec:weights} carries the proximal mapping over
to that vector, by reading the scalar characterization on the gains shifted by their corresponding
\(\lambda_i\). The aim of this paper is a closed form for the proximal calculus
of the resulting regularizer that can be used as a black box inside
forward--backward methods \cite{moreau1962,beckbook}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
The main result is a characterization of the proximal mapping of this
regularizer, extending \cite[Theorem~3.2]{beck2018} from an upper bound alone
to a window on the number of active groups. A lower bound makes the mapping
sensitive to whether the origin belongs to the group sets. We require each
\(D_i\) to exclude the origin; \cref{sec:emptyfix} shows that the mapping may
otherwise be empty even for closed groups. Under that hypothesis we
describe every proximal point and give an algorithm that computes one. The
penalty may depend on the group. \Cref{sec:weights} applies the same
characterization to the gains after subtracting twice the corresponding
penalty. Equal penalties are the case in which this shift preserves the order
of the gains.

\section{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

Fix a partition \(\{G_i\}_{i=1}^m\) of \(\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\) into \(m\) groups
of sizes \(n_1,\ldots,n_m\). After a harmless reindexing,
\[
\begin{aligned}
G_1 & \coloneqq\left\{1,2, \ldots, n_1\right\}, G_2\coloneqq\left\{n_1+1, n_1+2, \ldots, n_1+n_2\right\}, \ldots, \\
G_m & \coloneqq\left\{n_{m-1}+1, n_{m-1}+2, \ldots, n\right\} .
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(\x\in\R^n\) and \(T\subset \{1,\ldots,n\}\), write \(\x_T\) for the
subvector of \(\x\) indexed by \(T\). The map \(g: \R^n \to\{0,1\}^m\) that
marks nonempty groups is
$$
g(\x)_i\coloneqq \begin{cases}1, & \x_{G_i} \neq \mathbf{0}, \\
0, & \text{otherwise }\end{cases}.
$$
Thus \(g(\x)_i=1\) means that group \(G_i\) is \emph{active} for \(\x\),
otherwise it is \emph{inactive}. For \(l,s\in \N_0\) with \(0\leq l\leq s\leq m\),
$$
C_{l,s}\coloneqq\left\{\x \in \R^n:l\leq\|g(\x)\|_0 \leq s\right\}
$$
is the set of vectors with at least \(l\) and at most \(s\) active groups.
Ordinary (ungrouped) sparsity is the special case in which every group is a
singleton, so that \(C_{l,s}\) consists of the \(s\)-sparse vectors with at
least \(l\) nonzero entries.

The problem under study is
\begin{equation}
\tag{P0}
\label{P0}
\begin{aligned}
&\min&& f(\x)+\lambda\|g(\x)\|_0 \\
&\operatorname{s.t.}&& \x \in C_{l,s} \cap B,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(\lambda \geq 0\) is a penalty parameter, \(f: \R^n \to \R\) is
continuously differentiable and bounded from below, and
\begin{equation}
\label{B}
B \coloneqq \prod_{i=1}^m\left(D_i \cup\{\mathbf{0}\}\right)=\left(D_1 \cup\{\mathbf{0}\}\right) \times\left(D_2 \cup\{\mathbf{0}\}\right) \times \cdots \times\left(D_m \cup\{\mathbf{0}\}\right),
\end{equation}
with each \(D_i \subset \R^{n_i}\) nonempty and closed, and with
\(\mathbf{0}\notin D_i\). \Cref{sec:emptyfix} explains why the origin has to
be excluded. This problem is equivalent, in
composite unconstrained form, to
\begin{equation}
\label{P}
\min _{\x \in \R^n} f(\x)+h(\x),
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\label{h}
h(\x) \coloneqq\lambda\|g(\x)\|_0+\chi_{B \cap C_{l,s}}(\x).
\end{equation}
\Cref{sec:weights} replaces the common penalty \(\lambda\) by a penalty
\(\lambda_i\) on group \(i\). The arguments through \Cref{sec:closed} treat the
equal-penalty case \(\lambda_i=\lambda\).
Problem~\eqref{P} has the familiar shape of proximal composite optimization.
The proximal mapping of \(h\)
\begin{equation}
\label{prox}
\begin{aligned}
\prox_{h}(\x)&\coloneqq\argmin_{\U\in\R^n}\left\{h(\U)+ \frac{1}{2}\norm{\U-\x}^2 \right\}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
was characterized in \cite{beck2018} for \(l=0\). \Cref{thm:proxh} below
extends that characterization to \(0<l\leq s\).

\section{Origin exclusion and modelling}
\label{sec:emptyfix}

The hypothesis \(\mathbf{0}\notin D_i\) is part of the model, not an optional
regularization. If some \(D_i\) contains the origin, \(\prox_h\) may be empty
even though every \(D_i\) is closed.

\begin{example}[empty proximal mapping]
\label{ex:counter}
Let \(m=1\), with scalar group \(D_1=[0,+\infty)\), and take
\(l=s=1\). At \(\z=(0)\), the point \((t)\) with \(t>0\) lies in \(B\cap C_{l,s}\) and
the objective of \eqref{prox} equals
\[
\lambda+\frac12 t^2.
\]
The infimum of these values is \(\lambda\), approached as \(t\downarrow 0\), and
it is not attained. The limit is the origin, which has no active group, so it
lies outside \(C_{l,s}\). Hence the proximal mapping at the origin is empty.
\end{example}

What fails is that \(\mathbf{0}\in D_1\). The feasible block \((t)\) shrink
toward the origin, which is the inactive state of the first group, and the
infimum of the proximal objective is not attained. 

If instead each \(D_i\) is closed and excludes the origin, the distance from
\(\mathbf{0}\) to \(D_i\) is positive. The origin of group \(i\) is then
feasible only by switching the group off, through the factor \(\{\mathbf{0}\}\)
in \(B\). A nearest point of \(\x_{G_i}\) in \(D_i\) exists and is never
\(\mathbf{0}\), so every group can be turned on properly. For each admissible support
the nearest-point problem on the corresponding product of sets \(D_i\) attains
its minimum, the resulting blocks are nonzero, and \(h\) depends on the point
only through which groups are active. There are finitely many supports of
size between \(l\) and \(s\), so the minimum in \eqref{prox} is attained and
\(\prox_h(\x)\) is nonempty at every \(\x\).

A direct way to enforce the exclusion is to cut from each \(D_i\) the open
ball of radius \(\delta_i>0\) about the origin, in the Euclidean or
\(\ell_\infty\) norm,
\[
D_i^{\delta}\coloneqq D_i\setminus\bigl\{\z\in\R^{n_i}:\|\z\|<\delta_i\bigr\}.
\]
If \(\delta_i\) is small enough that \(D_i\) is not swallowed by the
ball, then \(D_i^{\delta}\) is closed and does not contain the origin.
The origin remains available through \(B\), so a group can still be switched
off, and an active group must leave the ball. The chosen radius should be a
meaningful scale of the model rather than a machine epsilon (see 
\autoref{sec:numerics} for further discussions).

The portfolio of \cref{ex:carteira} is already on the safe side of that
geometry as \(D_i=\Delta_{n_i}\) does not contain the origin, so every sector
can be turned on and \(\prox_h\) is nonempty. A different modelling issue remains.
A point of \(\Delta_{n_i}\) may still put an arbitrarily small weight on
some assets. In the same spirit as the hole about the origin, one may cut
each coordinate out of the open interval \((0,t)\), for instance \(t=0.05\):
\[
D_i^{t}
=\Delta_{n_i}\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{n_i}\bigl\{\z\in\R^{n_i}:\z_k\in(0,t)\bigr\}
=\bigl\{\z\in\Delta_{n_i}:\z_k\notin(0,t)\text{ for all }k\bigr\}.
\]
Note that \(D_i^{t}\) is still closed. The origin remains again available by switching the
whole group off through \(B\).

\section{Motivating examples}
\label{sec:examples}

\begin{example}[sector-constrained portfolio]
\label{ex:carteira}
Following \cite{beck2018}, consider mean--variance allocation with a bound on
the number of sectors that may receive capital,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mv}
\begin{aligned}
&\min&& \gamma \cdot \x^T \mathbf{C} \x-\boldsymbol{\mu}^T \x \\
&\operatorname{s.t.}&& \x \in C_{l,s} \cap B,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where each \(D_i\) is the probability simplex on sector \(i\),
\[
\Delta_{n_i}
=\bigl\{\z\in\R^{n_i}:\z\ge\mathbf{0},\ \mathbf{1}^\top\z=1\bigr\},
\]
so that
\(B=\prod_{i=1}^m(\Delta_{n_i}\cup\{\mathbf{0}\})\).
Here \(\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \R^n\) is a vector of expected returns,
\(\mathbf{C} \in \R^{n \times n}\) is a covariance matrix, and \(\gamma>0\) trades
off risk against return. An active sector has nonnegative weights that sum
to one on its own assets and an inactive sector is the origin. An
upper bound \(s\) caps how many sectors may be used and a positive lower bound \(l\)
enforces a minimal diversification
across them, in the same spirit as sparse Markowitz models
\cite{brodie2009}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[cardinality-constrained sparse regression]
\label{ex:imbuia}
An instance of \eqref{P} with singleton groups is
\begin{equation}
\label{regress2}
\min_{\x\in\R^n}\frac{1}{2}\|\A\x-\bv\|^2+\lambda\|\x\|_0+\chi_{C_{l,s}}(\x),
\end{equation}
with \(\A\in \R^{m\times n}\) and \(\bv\in \R^m\), and \(\chi\) being the set 
indicator function. The formulation was motivated by infrared spectroscopy on 
the IMBUI Abeamline (SIRIUS/LNLS), where a sparse spectrogram is recovered from
measurements.
If a prior estimate of the number of spectral lines is available --- from
previous experiments or from the literature --- it can be encoded as the
lower bound \(l\), so that the \(\ell_0\) penalty is not free to discard
known structure.
\end{example}

\section{Notation}
\label{sec:notation}

We first present a few auxiliary objects. Given \(\V \in \R^m\) and
\(i=1,2,\ldots,m\), \(\V_{[i]}\) denotes the \(i\)-th largest entry of
\(\V\), so that \(\V_{[1]}\geq \V_{[2]}\geq\hdots\geq\V_{[m]}\). The family
\begin{equation}\label{defS}
    \mathcal{S}_i(\V)\coloneqq\{S \cup T\mid S\coloneqq\{j\mid\V_j>\V_{[i]}\},\ T\subset\{j\mid\V_j=\V_{[i]}\},\ | S \cup T | = i\}\quad \forall i=1,2,\ldots,m
 \end{equation}
collects every index set of cardinality \(i\) associated with the largest
entries of \(\V\). The set \(\mathcal{S}_i(\V)\) need not be a singleton,
since \(\V\) may have ties. For instance, if \(\V = (0\ 0\ 1)^T\), then
\(\mathcal{S}_2(\V) = \{\{1,3\},\{2,3\}\}\), where
\(S=\{j\mid\V_j>\V_{[2]}\}=\{3\}\) and the admissible subsets of
\(\{j\mid\V_j=\V_{[2]}\}\) are \(T=\{1\}\) and \(T=\{2\}\).

Given \(\x \in \R^n\), the orthogonal projection onto a set \(C\subset\R^n\)
is
$$
P_C(\x)\coloneqq \prox_{\chi_C}(\x)=\argmin_{\y\in C}\|\y-\x\|_2,
$$
and the distance to \(D_i\) is
$$
d_{D_i}\left(\x_{G_i}\right)\coloneqq\min _{\z \in D_i}\left\|\x_{G_i}-\z\right\|_2\quad \forall i=1,2, \ldots, m .
$$
The latter is well defined because each \(D_i\) is closed. The gain map
\(\omega: \R^n \to \R^m\),
\begin{equation}
\label{omega}
\omega(\x)_i\coloneqq\left\|\x_{G_i}\right\|_2^2-d_{D_i}^2\left(\x_{G_i}\right)\quad \forall i=1,2, \ldots, m,
\end{equation}
governs which groups are activated by \(\prox_h\).

The support of a vector (its set of active-group indices) is
$$I_1(\x)\coloneqq\left\{i \in\{1,2, \ldots, m\}\mid g(\x)_i=1\right\}.$$
Since \(\mathbf{0}\notin D_i\), a nearest point of a block in \(D_i\) is never
the origin, so every group can be activated.
Level sets of the gain are written
$$I_{a}^{\stackrel{?}{=}}(\x)\coloneqq\left\{i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}\mid \omega(\x)_i\stackrel{?}{=}a\right\},$$
where \(\stackrel{?}{=}\) stands for an arbitrary relation, and \(a\in\R\) may
also be written as \([i]\) to mean \(\omega(\x)_{[i]}\).

For an index set \(T\subset\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\), the matrix \(\mathbf{U}_T\)
consists of the columns of \(\mathbf{I}_n\) indexed by \(T\), and we write
$$
\mathcal{A}(T)\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\in T} G_i
$$
and
$$
\mathcal{B}(T)\coloneqq \prod_{i\in T}D_i.
$$

The following identity is \cite[Lemma~3.1]{beck2018}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:toomega}
Let \(\x \in \R^n\) and \(T, S \subset\{1,2, \ldots, m\}\). Then, for all
\(\z, \y \in \R^n\) satisfying
$$
\z_{G_i} \in\left\{\begin{array} { l l }
{ P _ { D _ { i } } ( \x _ { G _ { i } } ) , } & { i \in T  } \\
{ \{ \mathbf { 0 } \} , } & { i \notin T  }
\end{array}, \quad \y _ { G _ { i } } \in \left\{\begin{array}{ll}
P_{D_i}\left(\x_{G_i}\right), & i \in S \\
\{\mathbf{0}\}, & i \notin S
\end{array}\right.\right.,
$$
one has
$$
\|\z-\x\|_2^2-\|\y-\x\|_2^2=\sum_{i \in S} \omega(\x)_i-\sum_{i \in T} \omega(\x)_i .
$$
\end{lemma}

\section{Characterization of the proximal mapping}
\label{sec:prox}

We now characterize \(\prox_{h}\) for variable \(l\). The argument follows
\cite[Theorem~3.2]{beck2018} closely: the same comparison of gains is used,
and the first half of the proof, corresponding to \(\omega(\x)_{[l]}> 2\lambda\),
is conceptually identical to the \(l=0\) case, up to a few details.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:proxh}
Let \(\x \in \R^n\). Then \(\prox_h(\x)\) is nonempty, and \(\U \in \prox_h(\x)\)
if and only if the following conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item \(\U_{G_i} \in P_{D_i}\left(\x_{G_i}\right)\ \forall i \in I_1(\U)\);
    \item If \(\omega(\x)_{[l]}> 2\lambda\) or \(l=0\), \(\exists T \in \mathcal{S}_{s}(\omega(\x))\) such that
\begin{equation}
\label{T1}
    T \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x) \subset I_1(\U) \subset T \cap I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x),
\end{equation}
and if \(\omega(\x)_{[l]}\leq 2\lambda\) and \(l\geq 1\), \(\exists T \in \mathcal{S}_{l-1}(\omega(\x))\), \(i\in I^=_{[l]}(\x)\setminus T\) and
$$
U\in
\begin{cases}
    \left\{W \subset I_{2 \lambda}^{=}(x) \backslash(T \cup\{i\})\mid |W| =\min \bigl\{\bigl|I_{2 \lambda}^=(\x)\setminus(T\cup\{i\})\bigr|, s-l\bigr\}\right\},&\omega(\x)_{[l]}=2\lambda\\
    \{\emptyset\},&\omega(\x)_{[l]}<2\lambda
\end{cases}
$$
such that
\begin{equation}
\label{T2}
T\cup \{i\}\subset I_1(\U)\subset T\cup\{i\}\cup U.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Nonemptiness is the argument of \cref{sec:emptyfix}.
Suppose that \(\U\) satisfies (a) and (b), and let
\(\y \in \prox_{h}(\x)\).
We will show that \(\U \in \prox_h(\x)\) and that \(\y\)
satisfies (a) and \eqref{T1} or \eqref{T2} (as appropriate) for some
\(\tilde{T}\) and, if needed, \(\tilde{U}\), defined as in (b). Note that, by
(a), \(\U\in B\), and by (b), \(\U\in C_{l,s}\) (this will be checked in more
detail for each case). Therefore, since \(\y \in \prox_h(\x)\)
(hence also \(\y\in B\cap C_{l,s}\)),
\begin{equation}
\label{proxy}
    2 \lambda\left(\|g(\U)\|_0-\|g(\y)\|_0\right)+\|\U-\x\|_2^2-\|\y-\x\|_2^2 \geq 0 .
\end{equation}
Plainly, \(\y_{G_i} \in P_{D_i}\left(\x_{G_i}\right)\) \(\forall i \in I_1(\y)\)
(otherwise a better solution of the problem defining
\(\prox_h(\x)\) could be obtained with the same active groups)
and, by hypothesis, \(\U_i \in P_{D_i}\left(\x_{G_i}\right)\ \forall i \in I_1(\U)\).
Hence, by \Cref{lem:toomega}, \eqref{proxy} is the same as
$$
\sum_{i \in I_1(\y)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)-\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) \geq 0,
$$
which in turn is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
\label{main1}
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)+\sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I^<_{2\lambda}(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)\\
&\qquad-\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) \geq 0 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

We first treat the first case of (b). The case \(l=0\) is
\cite[Theorem~3.2]{beck2018}. If \(l\geq 1\), \eqref{T1} indeed yields
\(l\leq |I_1(\U)|\leq s\), since \(|T \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)|\geq l\)
because \(\omega(\x)_{[l]}> 2\lambda\), and \(|T|=s\) by \eqref{defS}.

Any indices of \(I_1(\U)\) that lie in \(I_{2\lambda}^=(\x)\) do not change the sum, hence by \eqref{T1}
$$
\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)=\sum_{i \in T \cap I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)=\sum_{i \in T \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) .
$$
Since \(T\) contains indices of a set of the \(s\) largest entries of
\(\omega(\x)\) and \(\y\) has at most \(s\) active groups (because
\(\y \in C_{l,s}\)), one obtains
$$
\sum_{i \in T \cap I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) \geq \sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) .
$$
Combining the last two relations yields
\begin{equation}
\label{ubest}
\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) \geq \sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) .
\end{equation}

Using the elementary inequality
\begin{equation}
\label{negat}
    \sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I^<_{2\lambda}(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) \leq 0
\end{equation}
together with \eqref{ubest} gives
\begin{equation}
    \label{desig}
\begin{aligned}
0 & \geq \sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)-\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) \\
& \geq \sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)+\sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I^<_{2\lambda}(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) \\
& \qquad-\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) \\
& \geq 0. \eqjust{main1}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus the chain \eqref{desig} holds as a chain of equalities, and therefore
\eqref{proxy} holds as an equality as well, which implies
\(\U \in \prox_h(\x)\) by the definition \eqref{prox}. The fact
that \eqref{desig} is a chain of equalities also implies
$$
\sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)=\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)
$$
and
$$
\sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I^<_{2\lambda}(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)=0,
$$
which is equivalent to \(I_1(\y) \cap I^<_{2\lambda}(\x)=\emptyset\) by
negativity of the summands. The last two relations in turn imply
$$
\sum_{i \in I_1(\y)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)=\sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)=\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) .
$$

It remains to exhibit \(\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{S}_{s}(\omega(\x))\) such that
\eqref{T1} holds with \(\U\) replaced by \(\y\).
One already has \(I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)=\emptyset\),
\(l\leq |I_1(\y)|\leq s\)
and by the above equation
\[
\sum_{i\in I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)}\bigl(\omega(\x)_i-2\lambda\bigr)=\sum_{i\in T\cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)}\bigl(\omega(\x)_i-2\lambda\bigr).
\]
The terms in \(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\) are strictly positive and those in
\(I_{2\lambda}^=(\x)\) vanish.

If \(\omega(\x)_{[s]}>2\lambda\), then \(T\cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)=T\) has
cardinality \(s\) and realizes the maximal sum among subsets of \(s\)
indices. A proper subset of \(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\), or the swap of an index
in \(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\) for one in \(I_{2\lambda}^=(\x)\), strictly
decreases the sum. Hence \(I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\in\mathcal{S}_{s}(\omega(\x))\)
and \(|I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)|=s\), which forces
\(I_1(\y)\subset I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\). Take \(\tilde{T}\coloneqq I_1(\y)\). Then
\(\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{S}_{s}(\omega(\x))\) and
\(\tilde{T}\cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)=I_1(\y)=\tilde{T}\cap I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x)\).

If \(\omega(\x)_{[s]}=2\lambda\), every set in \(\mathcal{S}_{s}(\omega(\x))\)
contains \(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\), hence
\(T\cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)=I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\). Equality of the positive
sums forces \(I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)=I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\). Together with
\(I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)=\emptyset\), this yields
\(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\subset I_1(\y)\subset I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x)\). Since
\(|I_1(\y)|\leq s\) and \(|I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x)|\geq s\), there exists
\(W\subset I_{2\lambda}^=(\x)\) with \(I_1(\y)\setminus I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\subset W\)
and \(|W|=s-|I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)|\). Take
\(\tilde{T}\coloneqq I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\cup W\). Then
\(\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{S}_{s}(\omega(\x))\) and
\(\tilde{T}\cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\subset I_1(\y)\subset \tilde{T}\cap I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x)\).

If \(\omega(\x)_{[s]}<2\lambda\), again
\(T\cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)=I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\) and equality of the positive
sums gives \(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\subset I_1(\y)\subset I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x)\). Any
\(\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{S}_{s}(\omega(\x))\) satisfies
\(\tilde{T}\cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)=I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\) and
\(\tilde{T}\cap I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x)=I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x)\), so \eqref{T1}
holds for \(\y\).

In all subcases, \(|I_1(\y)|\geq l\): if \(\omega(\x)_{[s]}>2\lambda\) one has
\(|I_1(\y)|=s\geq l\) and, in the others, \(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\subset I_1(\y)\) and
\(|I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)|\geq l\) because \(\omega(\x)_{[l]}>2\lambda\) (or
\(l=0\)).
Finally, as already observed, \(\y_{G_i} \in P_{D_i}\left(\x_{G_i}\right)\)
\(\forall i \in I_1(\y)\), so (a) also holds for \(\y\).

We now treat the second case of (b), in which \(\omega(\x)_{[l]}\leq 2\lambda\).
Indeed, \eqref{T2} gives \(l\leq |I_1(\U)|\leq s\), since
\(|T\cup \{i\}|=l\) by \eqref{defS}, and \(|T\cup\{i\}\cup U|\leq s\) because
\(|U|\leq s-l\) by construction of \(U\).

If \(\omega(\x)_{[l]}=2\lambda\), one has \(I_{[l]}^>(\x)=I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\)
and \(|I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)|\leq l-1\). Since \(T\in\mathcal{S}_{l-1}(\omega(\x))\),
every index of \(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\) is among the \(l-1\) largest, hence
\(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\subset T\). By \eqref{T2},
\(I_1(\U)\setminus I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\subset I_{2\lambda}^=(\x)\), and therefore
\(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\subset I_1(\U)\subset I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x)\). The terms
in \(I_{2\lambda}^=(\x)\) vanish, whence
\[
\sum_{i\in I_1(\U)}\bigl(\omega(\x)_i-2\lambda\bigr)=\sum_{i\in I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)}\bigl(\omega(\x)_i-2\lambda\bigr)\geq\sum_{i\in I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)}\bigl(\omega(\x)_i-2\lambda\bigr).
\]
Together with \eqref{negat} and \eqref{main1}, the chain \eqref{desig} holds
again and is satisfied as equality. Thus \(\U\in\prox_h(\x)\),
\(I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)=\emptyset\), and equality of the positive
sums forces \(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\subset I_1(\y)\). One obtains
\(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\subset I_1(\y)\subset I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x)\) and, since
\(\y\in C_{l,s}\), \(l\leq |I_1(\y)|\leq s\).

It remains to exhibit \(\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{S}_{l-1}(\omega(\x))\),
\(\tilde{\imath}\in I^=_{[l]}(\x)\setminus\tilde{T}\) and \(\tilde{U}\) as in
\eqref{T2} such that \eqref{T2} holds with \(\U\) replaced by \(\y\). Since
\(|I_1(\y)|\geq l>|I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)|\), the set \(I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^=(\x)\) is
nonempty. Pick \(\tilde{\imath}\) in that set and
\(\tilde{T}\subset I_1(\y)\setminus\{\tilde{\imath}\}\) containing
\(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\) with \(|\tilde{T}|=l-1\). Then
\(\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{S}_{l-1}(\omega(\x))\) and
\(\tilde{\imath}\in I^=_{[l]}(\x)\setminus\tilde{T}\). The residual
\(I_1(\y)\setminus(\tilde{T}\cup\{\tilde{\imath}\})\) lies in
\(I_{2\lambda}^=(\x)\setminus(\tilde{T}\cup\{\tilde{\imath}\})\) and has
cardinality \(|I_1(\y)|-l\leq s-l\). Hence there exists
\(\tilde{U}\subset I_{2\lambda}^=(\x)\setminus(\tilde{T}\cup\{\tilde{\imath}\})\)
with \(I_1(\y)\setminus(\tilde{T}\cup\{\tilde{\imath}\})\subset\tilde{U}\) and
\(|\tilde{U}|=\min\bigl\{\bigl|I_{2\lambda}^=(\x)\setminus(\tilde{T}\cup\{\tilde{\imath}\})\bigr|,s-l\bigr\}\).
Therefore
\(\tilde{T}\cup\{\tilde{\imath}\}\subset I_1(\y)\subset\tilde{T}\cup\{\tilde{\imath}\}\cup\tilde{U}\).
As already observed, \(\y_{G_i} \in P_{D_i}\left(\x_{G_i}\right)\)
\(\forall i \in I_1(\y)\), so (a) also holds for \(\y\).

Now suppose \(\omega(\x)_{[l]}<2\lambda\). By (b) and \eqref{T2},
\(I_1(\U)=T\cup\{i\}\in\mathcal{S}_l(\omega(\x))\) has cardinality \(l\),
hence \(I_1(\U)\cap I_{[l]}^<(\x)=\emptyset\), and
\begin{equation}
\label{ubest2}
\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)=\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x) }\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)+\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^\geq(\x) }\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right).
\end{equation}

By (b) again, since \(T\) contains indices of a set of the \(l-1\) largest
entries of \(\omega(\x)\), one has \(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\subset I_1(\U)\), and
then
\begin{equation}
    \label{greaterind}
    |I_1(\U) \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)|=|I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)|\geq |I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)|.
\end{equation}
The same reasoning yields \(I^=_{2\lambda}(\x)\subset I_1(\U)\) and therefore
\begin{equation}
    \label{greaterind2}
    |I_1(\U) \cap I_{2\lambda}^{=}(\x)|=|I_{2\lambda}^{=}(\x)|\geq |I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^{=}(\x)|.
\end{equation}
In particular, from \eqref{greaterind} and positivity of the summands,
\begin{equation}
\label{2lineq}
\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) \geq \sum_{i \in I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right).
\end{equation}

Since by construction in this case \(|I_1(\U)|= l\) (\(U=\emptyset\) in
\eqref{T2}) and \(|I_1(\y)|\geq l\) (\(\y\in C_{l,s}\)), one has
\begin{align*}
    |I_1(\U) \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)|+|I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^\leq(\x)|
    &\geq|I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)|+|I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^\leq(\x)| \eqjust{greaterind}\\
    &=|I_1(\y)|
    \\&\geq |I_1(\U)|
    \\&=|I_1(\U) \cap I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)|+|I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^\leq(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^\geq(\x)| ,
\end{align*}
or equivalently,
\begin{equation}
    \label{inddiff}
    |I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^\leq(\x)|\geq |I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^\leq(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^\geq(\x)|.
\end{equation}

Since
\(|I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)|=|\bigl(I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^\leq(\x)\bigr)|-|\bigl(I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^=(\x)\bigr)|\)
(and likewise for \(\U\)), the inequality above on the first term and
\eqref{greaterind2} on the second yield in particular
\begin{equation}
    \label{inddiff2}
    |I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)|\geq |I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^\geq(\x)|.
\end{equation}
That is, \(I_1(\y)\) may have to include indices from \(I_{[l]}^\leq(\x)\),
because
\(I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^>(\x)=I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^>(\x)\)
by construction (\(T\in\mathcal{S}_{l-1}(\omega (\x))\) and
\(T\subset I_1(\U)\)). One therefore obtains
\begin{equation}
\label{full}
\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^\geq(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)
\geq \sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)
\eqjust{inddiff2},
\end{equation}
where it was also used that
\(\omega(\x)_{i}\geq \omega(\x)_j\ \forall i\in I^\geq_{[l]}(\x),\ j\in I^\leq_{[l]}(\x)\).

One may now form the chain of inequalities
\begin{equation}\label{lastchain}
\begin{aligned}
0 & \geq \sum_{i \in I_1(\y)\cap I_{2 \lambda}^>(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)-\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)\cap I_{2 \lambda}^>(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)
\eqjust{2lineq} \\
& =\sum_{i \in I_1(\y)\cap I_{2 \lambda}^\geq(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)-\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)\cap I_{2 \lambda}^\geq(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) \\
& \geq \sum_{i \in I_1(\y)\cap I_{2 \lambda}^\geq(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)+\sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I^<_{2 \lambda}(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)
\eqjust{full} \\
& \qquad-\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)\cap I_{2 \lambda}^\geq(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)- \sum_{i \in I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^\geq(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)\\
& =\sum_{i \in I_1(\y)\cap I_{2 \lambda}^\geq(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)+\sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I^<_{2 \lambda}(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)
\eqjust{ubest2} \\
& \qquad-\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right) \\
& \geq 0. \eqjust{main1}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Hence the chain holds with equality, which implies, for the second term in
particular,
\begin{equation}
    \label{greatereq}
\sum_{i \in I_1(\y)\cap I_{2 \lambda}^>(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)=\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)\cap I_{2 \lambda}^>(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right),
\end{equation}
and consequently, since \(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\subset I_1(\U)\),
\begin{equation}\label{greatereq2}
    I_1(\y)\cap I_{2 \lambda}^>(\x)=I_1(\U)\cap I_{2 \lambda}^>(\x).
\end{equation}

For the last term of \eqref{lastchain}, \eqref{greatereq} also yields
(omitting the vanishing summands on \(I_{2\lambda}^=(\x)\))
\begin{equation}
\label{forcedeq}
\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^\geq(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)= \sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right).
\end{equation}
Since
\(I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^>(\x)=I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^>(\x)\),
the case
\(|I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)|> |I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^\geq(\x)|\)
would imply
\(|I_1(\y) \cap I_{[l]}^\leq(\x)|>|I_1(\U) \cap I_{[l]}^=(\x)|\), that is,
\(\y\) would need smaller elements than \(\U\) in the sum in order to obtain
\(|I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)|> |I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^\geq(\x)|\).
In that event \eqref{full} would hold as a strict inequality. By contradiction
with the equality \eqref{forcedeq}, one concludes
\(|I_1(\y) \cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)|= |I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^\geq(\x)|\).
Again from \eqref{forcedeq}, this implies
$$
\sum_{i \in I_1(\y) \cap I^<_{[l]}(\x)}\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)=0.
$$
Moreover, the latter relation and the negativity of the summands on
\(I_1(\y) \cap I^<_{[l]}(\x)\) guarantee
\begin{equation}\label{ylesser}
    I_1(\y) \cap I^<_{[l]}(\x)=\emptyset.
\end{equation}
In view of the last display, \eqref{greatereq2} and \(|I_1(\y)|\geq l\), and
recalling that \(I^=_{2\lambda}(\x)\subset I_1(\U)\), one is left with
\begin{equation}\label{yequals}
\begin{aligned}
|I_1(\y) \cap I^=_{2\lambda}(\x)|
&= |I_1(\U) \cap I^=_{2\lambda}(\x)|
= |I^=_{2\lambda}(\x)|, \\
I_1(\y) \cap I^=_{2\lambda}(\x)
&= I_1(\U) \cap I^=_{2\lambda}(\x)
= I^=_{2\lambda}(\x).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Using \eqref{greatereq2}, \eqref{ylesser} and \eqref{yequals}, the sum over
\(I_1(\y)\) reduces to
$$
\sum_{i \in I_1(\y) }\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)=\sum_{i \in I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x) }\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)+\sum_{i \in I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^\geq(\x) }\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right),
$$
which finally produces the chain of equalities
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i \in I_1(\y) }\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)&=
    \sum_{i \in I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x) }\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)+\sum_{i \in I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^\geq(\x) }\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)\\
    &=\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x) }\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)+\sum_{i \in I_1(\y)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^\geq(\x) }\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)
    \eqjust{greatereq}\\
    &=\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^\geq(\x) }\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)+\sum_{i \in I_1(\U)\cap I_{2\lambda}^<(\x)\cap I_{[l]}^\geq(\x) }\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right)
    \eqjust{forcedeq}\\
    &=\sum_{i \in I_1(\U) }\left(\omega(\x)_i-2 \lambda\right). \eqjust{ubest2}
\end{align*}

Finally, \eqref{proxy} also holds as an equality, which implies
\(\U \in \prox_h(\x)\).
It remains to exhibit \(\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{S}_{l-1}(\omega(\x))\),
\(\tilde{\imath}\in I^=_{[l]}(\x)\setminus\tilde{T}\) and
\(\tilde{U}=\emptyset\) such that \eqref{T2} holds with \(\U\) replaced by
\(\y\). From \eqref{greatereq2}, \eqref{ylesser}
and \eqref{yequals},
\(I_{2\lambda}^>(\x)\cup I_{2\lambda}^=(\x)\subset I_1(\y)\subset I_{[l]}^\geq(\x)\)
and \(|I_1(\y)|\geq l\). If \(|I_1(\y)|>l\), some index of \(I_1(\y)\) would have
\(\omega(\x)_i-2\lambda<0\) and could be removed, contradicting equality of
the sums with \(I_1(\U)\). Hence \(|I_1(\y)|=l\). If there existed
\(j\in I_{[l]}^>(\x)\setminus I_1(\y)\), some \(k\in I_1(\y)\cap I^=_{[l]}(\x)\) would
satisfy \(\omega(\x)_k=\omega(\x)_{[l]}<\omega(\x)_j\); the swap would
increase the sum, again a contradiction. Hence \(I_{[l]}^>(\x)\subset I_1(\y)\) and
\(I_1(\y)\in\mathcal{S}_l(\omega(\x))\). Since \(|I_{[l]}^>(\x)|\leq l-1\), the set
\(I_1(\y)\cap I^=_{[l]}(\x)\) is nonempty. Pick \(\tilde{\imath}\) in that set,
\(\tilde{T}\coloneqq I_1(\y)\setminus\{\tilde{\imath}\}\) and
\(\tilde{U}\coloneqq\emptyset\). Then
\(\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{S}_{l-1}(\omega(\x))\),
\(\tilde{\imath}\in I^=_{[l]}(\x)\setminus\tilde{T}\) and
\(I_1(\y)=\tilde{T}\cup\{\tilde{\imath}\}\).
As already discussed, \(\y\) also satisfies (a), which completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{Closed form and a computational scheme}
\label{sec:closed}

The previous result yields an explicit description of \(\prox_h\). One has
$$
\prox_h(\x)=\left\{\mathbf{U}_{\mathcal{A}(T)} \y\mid \y \in P_{\mathcal{B}(T)}\left(\x_{\mathcal{A}(T)}\right),\ T\in\mathcal{T}(\x)\right\},
$$
where
$$
\mathcal{T}(\x)=
\begin{cases}\left\{T\mid I_{[s]}^>({\x}) \subsetneq T \subset I_{[s]}^\geq({\x}),\ |T|=s\right\}, &\ \omega(\x)_{[l]}>2 \lambda,\ \omega(\x)_{[s]}>2 \lambda \\
\left\{T\mid I_{[s]}^>({\x}) \subset T \subset I_{[s]}^\geq({\x}),\ |T| \leq s\right\}, &\ \omega(\x)_{[l]}>2 \lambda,\ \omega(\x)_{[s]}=2 \lambda \\
\left\{T\mid I_{2\lambda}^>({\x}) \subset T \subset I_{2\lambda}^\geq({\x}),\ |T| < s\right\}, &\ \omega(\x)_{[l]}>2 \lambda,\ \omega(\x)_{[s]}<2 \lambda
\\\left\{T\mid I_{[l]}^>({\x}) \subsetneq T \subset I_{[l]}^\geq({\x}),\ l\leq|T| \leq s\right\}, &\ \omega(\x)_{[l]}=2 \lambda  \\
\left\{T\mid I_{[l]}^>({\x}) \subsetneq T \subset I_{[l]}^\geq({\x}),\ |T|=l\right\}, &\ \omega(\x)_{[l]}<2 \lambda
\end{cases}.
$$
This description suggests the following procedure for computing one element of
\(\prox_h\). It selects \(\mathcal{S}_{s}(\omega(\x))\) or
\(\mathcal{S}_l(\omega(\x))\) and returns the corresponding groupwise projection.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Group-sparse proximal mapping}
\label{alg:proxh}
\Input{\(\x\in\R^n\),\ \(l\leq s\leq m\) and \(\lambda \in \R\)}\\
\Output{\(\U\in\prox_h(\x)\)}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \State compute \(S\in \mathcal{S}_{s}(\omega(\x))\) (arbitrarily chosen)
\If{\(\omega(\x)_{[l]} >2\lambda\)}
\State \(T\gets\{i\in S\mid\omega(\x)_i >2\lambda\}\)
\Else
\State use \(S\) to compute \(T\in \mathcal{S}_l(\omega(\x))\)
\EndIf
\State \(\U\gets \mathbf{U}_{\mathcal{A}(T)} P_{\mathcal{B}(T)}\left(\x_{\mathcal{A}(T)}\right)\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{remark}
If \(\omega(\x)_{[l]} \leq 2\lambda\), it is enough to compute a selection
\(T\in\mathcal{S}_l(\omega(\x))\) of \(l\) of the largest entries of
\(\omega(\x)\), which is cheaper than an initial selection
\(S\in\mathcal{S}_s(\omega(\x))\) when \(l<s\). Moreover, entries with
\(\omega(\x)_i \leq 2\lambda\) are irrelevant when
\(\omega(\x)_{[l]} > 2\lambda\), so \(S\) can sometimes be shortened. 

A more refined implementation avoids sorting any entry that cannot change the support.
When \(s=m\), one pass keeps every index with \(\omega(\x)_i>2\lambda\) and records
the indices tied at \(2\lambda\). If at least \(l\) groups lie strictly above the
threshold, that set is a proximal support. Otherwise the ties are added. If the
set is still shorter than \(l\), the \(l-|T|\) largest entries strictly below
\(2\lambda\) are selected and added. If
\(l=0\) as well, the pass stops at the strict positives and nothing is sorted.
When \(l=0\) and \(s<m\), only entries above \(2\lambda\) are eligible. If there
are at most \(s\) of them they are all kept; otherwise the smallest
\(|\{i:\omega(\x)_i>2\lambda\}|-s\) of them are discarded. 
See \autoref{sec:complexity} for a more in depth analysis of an efficient implementation.

When evaluating \(\omega(\x)\) in \eqref{omega}, the projection onto each
\(D_i\) computed for \(d_{D_i}\) should be stored and reused in
\(P_{\mathcal{B}(T)}\).
\end{remark}

\section{Group-dependent penalties}
\label{sec:weights}

The regularizer \eqref{h} charges every active group the same penalty. The
proximal mapping extends to penalties that depend on the group. For
\(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m\geq 0\), set
\begin{equation}
\label{hlambda}
h_{\lambda}(\x)\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i\, g(\x)_i+\chi_{B\cap C_{l,s}}(\x).
\end{equation}
The regularizer \eqref{h} is the special case \(\lambda_i=\lambda\). Define the
shifted gain \(\rho(\x)\in\R^m\) by
\begin{equation}
\label{rho}
\rho_i(\x)\coloneqq\omega(\x)_i-2\lambda_i, \qquad i=1,\ldots,m.
\end{equation}
Its order statistics, and the sets \(\mathcal{S}_k(\rho(\x))\) built from them,
are those of \Cref{sec:notation} applied to the vector \(\rho(\x)\).

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:weights}
Let \(\x\in\R^n\). Then \(\prox_{h_{\lambda}}(\x)\) is nonempty. A vector
\(\U\) lies in \(\prox_{h_{\lambda}}(\x)\) if and only if it satisfies the
conditions of \Cref{thm:proxh} with \(\omega(\x)\) replaced by \(\rho(\x)\) and
with the threshold \(2\lambda\) replaced by \(0\). The same substitution
translates the description of \(\mathcal{T}(\x)\) in \Cref{sec:closed}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Nonemptiness is the argument of \Cref{sec:emptyfix}. It uses
\(\mathbf{0}\notin D_i\) and does not depend on the penalties. Let \(\U\) and
\(\y\) satisfy condition~(a) of \Cref{thm:proxh}, and write \(I\coloneqq I_1(\U)\)
and \(J\coloneqq I_1(\y)\). \Cref{lem:toomega} gives
\[
\|\U-\x\|_2^2-\|\y-\x\|_2^2=\sum_{i\in J}\omega(\x)_i-\sum_{i\in I}\omega(\x)_i.
\]
If \(\y\in\prox_{h_{\lambda}}(\x)\) and \(\U\) is feasible for \(h_{\lambda}\),
the proximal inequality, multiplied by two, now reads
\[
2\Bigl(\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_i-\sum_{i\in J}\lambda_i\Bigr)+\|\U-\x\|_2^2-\|\y-\x\|_2^2\geq 0.
\]
Substituting the identity above rearranges it to
\begin{equation}
\label{proxy-rho}
\sum_{i\in J}\rho_i(\x)-\sum_{i\in I}\rho_i(\x)\geq 0,
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{proxy} with the summands \(\omega(\x)_i-2\lambda\) replaced by
\(\rho_i(\x)\). From this line on, the proof of \Cref{thm:proxh} uses those
summands only through their order statistics, and through the fact that a
vanishing summand does not change the sum. Where that argument compares
\(\omega(\x)_i-2\lambda\) with \(\omega(\x)_j-2\lambda\), the same lines compare
\(\rho_i(\x)\) with \(\rho_j(\x)\), and a shifted gain equal to zero may be kept
or dropped. Both properties hold for \(\rho(\x)\) with threshold \(0\). The case distinction on
the sign of \(\rho(\x)_{[l]}\) therefore replaces the case distinction on
\(\omega(\x)_{[l]}-2\lambda\), and every set built from \(\omega(\x)\) and from
the level \(2\lambda\) is replaced by the corresponding set built from
\(\rho(\x)\) and from the level \(0\).

If \(\lambda_i=\lambda\) for every \(i\), then \(\rho_i(\x)=\omega(\x)_i-2\lambda\).
The order of \(\rho(\x)\) agrees with the order of \(\omega(\x)\), and
\(\rho_i(\x)>0\) if and only if \(\omega(\x)_i>2\lambda\). The statement
reduces to \Cref{thm:proxh}.
\end{proof}

The order of \(\omega(\x)\) decides the support of \(h_{\lambda}\) only in that
equal-penalty case. A group with a large geometric gain and a large penalty
can have a smaller shifted gain than a group with a modest geometric gain and
a small penalty, so the set returned by \Cref{alg:proxh} need not be a
proximal support of \(h_{\lambda}\).

\begin{example}
\label{ex:unequal}
Take two groups, \(l=0\), \(s=1\), \(\omega(\x)=(5,4)\) and
\((\lambda_1,\lambda_2)=(1,0.1)\). Both geometric gains exceed twice the
penalty of their own group, since \(5>2\) and \(4>0.2\), and the larger entry
of \(\omega(\x)\) is the first group. The shifted gains are
\(\rho(\x)=(3,3.8)\), so a proximal point activates the second group instead.
\end{example}

\Cref{alg:proxh} is unchanged in the projection it returns. It changes in the
vector it sorts and the threshold it reads: the order statistics and the
test against \(2\lambda\) are taken on \(\rho(\x)\), against zero.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Group-sparse proximal mapping with group-dependent penalties}
\label{alg:proxhlam}
\Input{\(\x\in\R^n\),\ \(l\leq s\leq m\) and \(\lambda_i\geq 0\)}\\
\Output{\(\U\in\prox_{h_{\lambda}}(\x)\)}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \State compute \(\rho_i(\x)\gets \omega(\x)_i-2\lambda_i\) for \(i=1,\ldots,m\)
    \State compute \(S\in \mathcal{S}_{s}(\rho(\x))\) (arbitrarily chosen)
\If{\(\rho(\x)_{[l]} >0\)}
\State \(T\gets\{i\in S\mid \rho_i(\x) >0\}\)
\Else
\State use \(S\) to compute \(T\in \mathcal{S}_l(\rho(\x))\)
\EndIf
\State \(\U\gets \mathbf{U}_{\mathcal{A}(T)} P_{\mathcal{B}(T)}\left(\x_{\mathcal{A}(T)}\right)\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

When \(\lambda_i=\lambda\) for every \(i\), one has
\(\mathcal{S}_{k}(\rho(\x))=\mathcal{S}_{k}(\omega(\x))\) and
\(\{\,i:\rho_i(\x)>0\,\}=\{\,i:\omega(\x)_i>2\lambda\,\}\), so
\Cref{alg:proxhlam} is analogous to \Cref{alg:proxh}.

\subsection{Complexity}
\label{sec:complexity}

The bound is for the procedure in the remark after \Cref{alg:proxh}, read on
the shifted gains of \Cref{alg:proxhlam}. The equal-penalty map is the same
arithmetic on \(\omega(\x)_i-2\lambda\). The cost below is the number of comparisons among the \(m\) scalars
\(\rho_i(\x)\).

Let \(w\) be the number of gains in the window that still contains the
requested rank. That rank is found by partitioning about a pivot taken
from a constant number of positions in the window. The search
continues only on the side that contains the rank. If the pivot equals the
gain immediately outside the window, one pass gathers every equal copy and
the search steps past that run, so a window of equal gains costs \(\Theta(w)\)
rather than one copy per partition. A cut that discards less than one eighth
of the current window exchanges a constant number of entries and continues.
Nothing stops a long chain of unbalanced cuts, so the worst case is
\(O(w^2)\) comparisons, the cost of a chain of extreme pivots in this
partition \cite[\S5.2.2]{knuth1998}. For a uniformly random order of the
window the expectation is \(\Theta(w)\): this is the bound for one pivot
position \cite[Ex.~5.2.2--32]{knuth1998}, and it remains linear when the
pivot is the median of a fixed number of sample positions
\cite{kirschenhofer1997,grubel1999}.

The support does not require every gain to be ordered. If \(s=m\), one pass
splits the gains into the strict positives, the zeros, and the strict
negatives, at cost \(\Theta(m)\). At least \(l\) strict positives, and in
particular \(l=0\), end the search: no order statistic. If the positives fall
short of \(l\) while the nonnegative gains do not, the support has cardinality
\(l\) and is read from that pass, including all positives and
\(l-\#positives\) zero gains. An order statistic is formed only when fewer
than \(l\) gains are nonnegative, and then only among those negatives with partition rank \(l-\#nonnegative\), at expected cost \(\Theta(\#negatives)\).

If \(l=0\) and \(s<m\), one pass collects the strict positives, at cost
\(\Theta(m)\). They are all kept when there are at most \(s\) of them. If
\(\#positives>s\), the order statistic of rank \(s\) is computed in the
positive block alone, at expected cost \(\Theta(\#positives)\). 

If \(0<l\leq s<m\), the order statistic of rank \(l\) is computed among all
\(m\) gains, at expected cost \(\Theta(m)\), and the other \(m-l\) entries
are left as they stand. The search stops when that \(l\)-th gain is
nonpositive or when \(l=s\). When it is positive and \(l<s\), one pass
collects the strict positives beyond it with cost \(\Theta(m-l)\). They are kept if they fit under
\(s\); otherwise the order statistic of rank \(s-l\) is taken only inside
that block of size \(q=\#positives-l\), at expected cost \(\Theta(q)\).

A call therefore uses a number of gain comparisons linear in \(m\) on
average, and equal to a single pass in every case that asks for no rank. The quadratic 
bound is confined to the window actually partitioned. As written, 
\Cref{alg:proxh,alg:proxhlam} begin with a
selection of rank \(s\). The method described above uses rank \(l\) whenever an
\(l\)-th gain is already nonpositive after an \(l\) order statistic or the sign split, when applicable. 
It also does not unnecessarily partition a window that cannot fully meet the support sign requirements, 
keeping the window as small as possible and only partitioning when necessary.

Those comparison counts are not the whole evaluation. Writing \(\U\) is a
further \(O(n)\) update where each coordinate is set, an inactive group to zero
and an active group to the projection already stored for it. The projection
of \(\x_{G_i}\) onto \(D_i\) is computed once while forming \(\rho(\x)\) and
is not counted above. Its cost depends on the sets \(D_i\) and is left out
of the bound.

\section{A sector portfolio computation}
\label{sec:numerics}

This section applies \cref{alg:proxh} to the sector portfolio of
\cref{ex:carteira} and, on the same returns, to a long-only Markowitz model
with a cap on the number of names. Prices are daily adjusted closes from
Yahoo Finance for the S\&P~500 constituents with a complete series on the
common window: 492 names in the 11 GICS sectors, as simple daily returns. The
panel has 417 sessions, from 5~May 2023 through 31~December 2024. The last
252 sessions, the calendar year 2024, are the test set. The preceding 165
sessions are the training window. The mean \(\boldsymbol{\mu}\) and the
covariance \(\mathbf{C}\) in \eqref{eq:mv} are the training sample mean and
the training sample covariance, with a ridge
\(10^{-4}\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{C})/n\) times the identity so that
\(\mathbf{C}\) is positive definite.

In the sector model each \(D_i\) is the simplex of sector \(i\) or the coordinatewise cutted version \(D_i^t\) when \(t>0\) (\cref{sec:emptyfix}), and
\(B=\prod_i(D_i\cup\{\mathbf{0}\})\). An active sector sums to one on its own
names and an inactive sector is the origin. The upper bound is \(s=11\) for all cases. The
runs use \(t\in\{0,0.05\}\) and, for \(\lambda>0\), \(l\in\{1,3,5\}\). The
case \(\lambda=0\) is solved once for each \(t\) with \(l=0\), as it usually results in solutions that don't hit the lower bound anyway. The Markowitz
model is the long-only simplex
\(\{\x\ge\mathbf{0}:\mathbf{1}^\top\x=1\}\) with \(\|\x\|_0\le k\) for
\(k\in\{1,5,10,20,40,80,492\}\). On that simplex an \(\ell_1\) penalty is
constant, so the cap is the sparsity control \cite{brodie2009}.

The coefficient \(\gamma\) in \eqref{eq:mv} is the positive number that
equates the risk term and the return term on a reference portfolio feasible
for that model. For the sector model the reference is the product of the
equal-weight simplices, one unit of capital in every sector, which gives
\(\gamma=1.35\). For the Markowitz model the reference is the equal-weight
portfolio of all 492 names, which gives \(\gamma=16.97\). Note that the second is about
eleven times the first, as rescaling the \(11\) simplices reference onto the unit
simplex divides the mean by approximetly \(11\) and the variance by \(11^2\). The tables give 
the held-out return, but the training value of \eqref{eq:mv} is not presented, since 
the two references used do not put that value on the same scale.

The sector penalty is a fraction of
\(\sigma=\frac12\|P(\x_{\mathrm{eq}})-\x_{\mathrm{eq}}\|_2^2\approx 0.131\),
where \(\x_{\mathrm{eq}}\) is the equal-weight vector of all names and \(P\)
is the projection onto the product of the uncut simplices. The positive
penalties are \(\lambda\in\{0.01,0.02,0.05,0.1\}\sigma\). For every \((t,l)\)
the two largest \(\lambda\) returned the same portfolio, and those pairs are one row.

Both models are solved by a nonmonotone GLL spectral proximal-gradient iteration
in the spirit of \cite{kanzow2022}, starting from the equal-weight book. For the
capped simplex the step is the Euclidean projection onto
\(\{\x\ge\mathbf{0}:\mathbf{1}^\top\x=1,\ \|\x\|_0\le k\}\), starting from
equal weights on the \(k\) names of largest training mean. The case \(k=1\)
compares \eqref{eq:mv} at the \(n\) vertices, because a projected step that
begins at one vertex does not reach another.

On the 252 test sessions the portfolio produces the daily series
\(r=\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{test}}\x\). The annualized mean is \(252\) times the
sample mean of \(r\), the annualized volatility is the sample standard
deviation of \(r\) times \(\sqrt{252}\), and the Sharpe ratio is their
quotient, with no risk-free rate. In the sector model, \(k\) active sectors
have gross exposure \(k\), so the annualized mean and volatility scale with
\(k\) while the Sharpe ratio does not. On a single test year the Sharpe
standard error is about one \cite{lo2002}. The remaining columns are the
number of active sectors, the number of names with positive weight, and the
smallest positive weight, which in the sector model is a share of a sector
that sums to one.

multi-sector rows. The gap between those rows and the equal-weight or
single-name rows is of the same order as the uncertainty of one test year.

\section{Concluding remarks}
\label{sec:end}

We have extended the proximal calculus of \cite{beck2018} from an upper
cardinality bound to a genuine window \(l\le\|g(\x)\|_0\le s\). The lower
bound changes the geometry of \(\prox_h\): when
\(\omega(\x)_{[l]}\le 2\lambda\) one must activate zero-gain (or even
negative-gain) groups in order to meet the quota \(l\). That phenomenon is
invisible when \(l=0\). Because each \(D_i\) excludes the origin, the mapping
itself is nonempty at every centre.

The resulting oracle is the proximal step of the spectral method in
\cref{sec:numerics}.

We do not recast the stationarity hierarchy of
\cite{beck2018} for the window \(C_{l,s}\). Once \(\prox_h\) is available those
conditions transfer formally.
